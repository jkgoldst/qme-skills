# Public sources the question frame rests on

These are the public California authorities behind the groups in `question-frame.md`. They explain why each group exists. The skill does not quote or interpret them in its output; quote a statute only after reading its current text.

## Labor Code section 3208.3 (psychiatric injury)

The compensability thresholds specific to psychiatric injury. The groups map to its elements:

- **Predominant cause, subdivision (b)(1).** Actual events of employment must be the predominant cause (more than half) of the psychiatric injury as to all causes combined. Groups 1, 2, and 4 gather the industrial and non-industrial causes the physician weighs.
- **Violent act, subdivision (b)(2).** A lower "substantial cause" threshold where the injury results from being a victim of a violent act or direct exposure to a significant violent act. Group 1 asks what happened.
- **Six months of employment, subdivision (d).** No compensation for a psychiatric injury unless the employee has been employed by that employer for at least six months, with an exception for a sudden and extraordinary employment condition. Group 2 asks the hire date and the last date worked.
- **Good-faith personnel action, subdivision (h).** No compensation where the injury was substantially caused by a lawful, nondiscriminatory, good-faith personnel action. Group 2 asks about every personnel action and the worker's account of it.

## Labor Code sections 4663 and 4664 (apportionment)

Apportionment of permanent disability to causation, including prior conditions and prior awards. Groups 3, 4, and 7 gather the prior psychiatric history, the non-industrial stressors, and the prior claims the physician needs before writing apportionment.

## Labor Code section 4628 (who takes the history)

The physician who signs the report is responsible for the history. The skill produces questions for the physician to ask; it does not take the history.

## California Code of Regulations, title 8, section 10682 (contents of medical-legal reports)

Formerly section 10606. Lists the elements a medical-legal report must contain, among them the history of the injury, the patient's complaints, the medical history including prior injuries, the disability, the causes of the disability, and the reasons for the opinion. The eight groups are organized so the interview yields each element.

## The QME regulations, title 8, sections 1 through 159

Govern the QME process, the evaluation, and the report. Relevant here: the evaluator reviews the records provided and takes a history from the injured worker in person.

## Diagnostic and rating references

The Global Assessment of Functioning scale and the 2005 Permanent Disability Rating Schedule are the rating references for psychiatric impairment in California. Group 5 gathers the function-by-function picture a GAF rests on. The rating itself is the physician's, after the exam; see `/qme-gaf-wpi` for the lookup.
